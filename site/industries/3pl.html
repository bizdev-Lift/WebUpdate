3PL

Your rates are your product. They have to be better than your client's.

A third-party logistics provider lives on the spread between the rates it buys and the rates it sells. If a prospect can get better pricing on their own, the pitch is over before it starts. Everything below is about protecting that spread.

Get a free rate analysis Talk to an expert

The products

Every client profile at once

A 3PL does not have a shipping profile. It inherits ten of them, and the platform has to handle all of them without a separate process each.

The market

Where this category sits online

Outsourced fulfilment continues to grow as brands decide warehousing is not their core competency. That growth brings competition, and in a crowded market rate access is one of the few things a 3PL can genuinely differentiate on.

Rates decide the deal

Fulfilment fees are broadly comparable across providers. The shipping rate a 3PL can offer is frequently what wins or loses the account.

Aggregated volume is the advantage

No single client has leverage. Combined across the book, a 3PL does — provided the rate programme is structured to recognise it.

Client-level visibility is expected

Brands want their own reporting, their own tracking experience and their own cost breakdown, not a line on your invoice.

Shipping profile

What actually drives your invoice

The commercial question for a 3PL is not just what shipping costs. It is what margin survives between the bought rate and the sold rate, per client.

The spread is the business

If you cannot see cost by client, by service and by surcharge type, you cannot know which accounts are carrying the others. That reporting is worth as much as the rate itself.
