Industrial and manufacturing

When the line is down, nobody asks what the freight costs.

Industrial shippers run two completely different shipping problems at once: routine MRO and replenishment that should be as cheap as possible, and just-in-time parts where a day of downtime costs more than a year of express premiums. Most agreements are built for only one of the two.

Get a free rate analysis Talk to an expert

The products

Dense parts, long stock, and urgent everything

The catalogue spans a wider weight and dimension range than almost any other category, which is precisely why a single default service costs money.

The market

Where this category sits online

B2B industrial distribution has moved online steadily, and MRO buyers now expect consumer-grade delivery visibility on parts that were traditionally phoned in. The shipping profile, though, has stayed industrial.

Two profiles, one account

Routine replenishment wants the cheapest ground service. Line-down parts want the fastest service available at any hour. Both run through the same agreement.

Downtime economics

The cost of a stopped production line dwarfs any express premium, which is why express spend in this category is rarely questioned and rarely optimised.

Delivery locations are difficult

Plants, yards, construction sites and remote facilities attract limited-access and delivery-area surcharges that consumer categories never see.

Shipping profile

What actually drives your invoice

The saving in this category almost never comes from paying less for urgent shipments. It comes from making sure only the genuinely urgent ones go out at express rates.

Express is a decision, not a habit

The single largest saving available to most industrial shippers is not a cheaper express rate. It is a rule that stops routine replenishment from riding an express service because that is what someone selected once.
