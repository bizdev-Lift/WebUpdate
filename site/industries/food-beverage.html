Food and beverage

Heavy, dense, perishable, and priced against free shipping.

Food and drink is the hardest category in e-commerce to ship profitably. The product is dense, the packaging adds weight rather than saving it, a large share of it is time-critical, and customers compare your delivery charge against a grocery store two miles away.

Get a free rate analysis Talk to an expert

The products

Three shipping problems in one catalogue

Shelf-stable, chilled and frozen are effectively three separate shipping operations that happen to share a warehouse.

The market

Where this category sits online

Food and beverage is now among the most-purchased categories online, with roughly a quarter of US consumers buying groceries or food products online in recent surveys. It is also the category where delivery cost most directly decides whether an order is profitable.

Ground rates decide the category

Dense product means high billable weight on every order. A percentage point on your ground discount matters more here than in any low-weight category.

Perishables force express

Chilled and frozen product cannot ride a five-day ground service. Transit time is a food safety requirement, not a service preference.

Cold chain adds billable weight

Gel packs, dry ice and insulated liners all add weight and cubic size to a shipment that was already dense.

Two very different cost problems, and an agreement that usually only addresses one of them.

Two levers, pulled separately

Shelf-stable volume needs the best possible ground rate. Perishable volume needs the right express service chosen by lane and transit time. Optimising one and ignoring the other is how most food shippers end up overpaying on both.

Right service ships

GroundBest negotiated rate
ExpressWhere transit time requires it
Compliant serviceAdult signature applied

  1. Transit-time rules by lane

    The service is chosen by how long the lane actually takes to that zone, so a perishable order to a nearby zone rides ground while a distant one goes express.

  2. Ship-day blocking

    Automatic rules that prevent a frozen shipment leaving on a day that would leave it sitting in a facility over the weekend.

  3. Packaging tied to the service chosen

    Coolant quantity driven by the transit time the rule selected, rather than packing every order for the worst case.

  4. Compliance built into routing

    Alcohol and other regulated products routed only to services and states where they are permitted, with adult signature applied automatically.
